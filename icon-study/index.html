Can everyone see the flame?

The green #78d600 against beige #eeecdf is only 1.55:1; the original brackets are 11.03:1. Softer brackets rebalance the emphasis, but do not increase flame contrast. C and D are my starting points; E and F are deliberately pale comparison endpoints.

Color-vision deficiency does not mean “green disappears.” Different types affect color distinctions differently, so this ratio cannot tell us what every person sees. It does flag weak brightness separation, especially for a tiny shape. See the National Eye Institute’s explanation.

WCAG uses 3:1 for meaningful graphical information, with exceptions for essential presentations such as logos. Here it is a useful design target, not a claim that this brand mark automatically fails WCAG. Small antialiased edges can be fainter still. If we want a more robust flame, the next experiment should change its brightness or add a contrasting edge—not just lighten the brackets.
